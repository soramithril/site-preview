    How it goes

    Four steps. You only do one of them.

    1. Book it

      Call, text or book online. We can usually drop a bin off the same day you call.

    2. We drop it

      On your driveway, with wood underneath so it doesn't mark the surface.

    3. You load it

      At your own pace. The 14 and 20 yard bins have a door at the back, so you can walk things in.

    4. We haul it

      You choose the pickup day when you book. Finished early? Call and we'll come sooner.

    Your driveway, drop off day

    A few minutes before the truck comes saves a lot of back and forth.

    • Pick the spot. Every bin is 12 ft long. If a car fits in the spot, our truck fits too.
    • Clear the way. Move cars out of the driveway on drop off and pickup day. The truck needs 9 ft of clear height, so watch for low wires and branches.
    • Load it smart. Flat, heavy things first, then the bulky ones. Load it level, with nothing above the top edge, and close the door before pickup day.

        Questions

        Good questions, straight answers.

        Still wondering? We'd rather you ask than guess. Call during business hours or text us anytime.

        705.333.7767
        Which bin size should I rent?
        Most homeowners go with our 14 yard bin. It's the right size for a garage or basement clean out, a small reno or a move. Some people upgrade to our 20 yard bin, the one our contractors use most for big jobs. Digging up the garden or breaking up an old patio? Our 4 and 7 yard bins are made for dirt and concrete. Still not sure? Tell us what you're clearing out and we'll help you pick. Our bin size guide has the details.
        How long can I keep my bin?
        As long as you need it. You choose the pickup day when you book, and we come and get the bin that day. Finished early? Give us a call and we'll pick it up sooner. Need more time? You can extend your rental for a fee. Long term rentals are available too.
        How does the dump fee work?
        The dump fee is $135 for up to 1 tonne (2,200 lb), prorated after that. It applies to the 14 and 20 yard bins. The 4 and 7 yard dirt and concrete bins are a flat rate with no dump fee.
        How do you protect my driveway?
        We've got wood. When our drivers drop a bin, they put wood underneath so it won't damage your driveway.
        Is there anything you can't take?
        In a bin, we can't take paint, oils or other liquids, chemicals, solvents, asbestos, oil drums, propane tanks, tires or medical waste. Most everything else, from appliances and furniture to drywall, concrete and yard waste, is fair game.
        How can I pay?
        We accept Visa, Mastercard, American Express, Visa Debit and e-Transfer. For bin rentals, we ask for a deposit when you book, starting at $450. The amount depends on your location, and we'll confirm it when we call.
        Is a bin the same as a dumpster or a garbage bin?
        Yes. Bin rental, dumpster rental, garbage bin rental and disposal bin rental all mean the same thing; around Barrie most people just say bin. We rent 4, 7, 14 and 20 yard bins, dropped on your driveway with wood underneath.
        How soon can you drop off a bin?
        Usually the same day you call, even in spring and summer.
        Do I need a permit for a bin?
        Not if it goes on your driveway. If it has to sit on the road, you need a road permit from your town, and getting it is up to you.
        Can roof shingles go in a bin?
        Yes, in a 14 yard bin. With shingles it's all about weight: a bundle weighs about 80 lb, and a double layer or heavier shingles weigh more, so tell us about the roof when you book. Roofing bins
        What if my bin is too heavy or overfilled?
        On the 14 and 20 yard bins, the dump fee covers up to 1 tonne (2,200 lb) and is prorated after that, so a heavier load costs a little more at the dump. The 4 and 7 yard dirt and concrete bins have no weight limit, as long as the load is clean: one material, nothing mixed in. Fill it no higher than the top edge, with nothing sticking out over the sides. An overfilled bin isn't safe to haul, so we can't take it until the load is level. Usually that just means moving a few things around to fill the gaps.
